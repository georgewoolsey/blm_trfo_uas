6.1 Group Tree Crowns

While some research on tree spatial patterns use tree point locations buffered with a fixed radius (e.g. 3 m) to approximate the crown radius of a “typical” overstory tree (e.g. Churchill et al. (2016)), we’ll use the irregular, more realistic crown shapes identified from the aerial data to determine tree clump groups.

Remember we are only looking at what could be considered “overstory” trees since we filtered the tree list to keep only trees that meet a certain minimum height threshold of 2 m

Since a primary objective of this analysis is to evaluate how different treatments influence forest structure, we’ll treat each unit as a discrete area and exclude any trees located outside of the unit boundaries from the clump classification process. This approach assumes that trees within a unit do not form functional clusters with those in adjacent areas (even though some units share a common border and we have data extending beyond the unit boundaries). This assumption prevents data from untreated control areas or differently treated areas from influencing the results when summarized by treatment type. This intentional ignorance of edge effects means that the scale of focus of this analysis is strictly on the treatment unit.

define a function to make the tree clump groupings by treatment unit

get_clumps_temp <- function(tree_list, buffer = 0) {
  # buffer and dissolve (arcGIS logic)
  clump_polys <- tree_list %>%
    dplyr::ungroup() %>% 
    sf::st_buffer(buffer) %>% 
    sf::st_union() %>%
    sf::st_cast("POLYGON") %>%
    sf::st_as_sf() %>%
    dplyr::mutate(clump_id = dplyr::row_number())

  # attach to trees
  trees_with_clumps <- tree_list %>%
    # get rid of columns we'll create
    dplyr::select( -dplyr::any_of(c(
      "hey_xxxxxxxxxx"
      , "clump_id"
      , "clump_n_trees"
      , "clump_n_trees_grp"
      , "clump_dist_m"
    ))) %>%
    sf::st_join(clump_polys %>% dplyr::select(clump_id), join = sf::st_intersects, largest = T) %>%
    dplyr::group_by(clump_id) %>%
    dplyr::mutate(
      clump_n_trees = dplyr::n()
      , clump_n_trees_grp = dplyr::case_when(
        clump_n_trees == 1 ~ "Individual"
        , clump_n_trees >= 2 & clump_n_trees <= 4 ~ "2-4 trees"
        
        , clump_n_trees >= 5 & clump_n_trees <= 10 ~ "5-10 trees"
        , clump_n_trees > 10 ~ ">10 trees"
        # , clump_n_trees >= 5 & clump_n_trees <= 9 ~ "5-9 trees"
        # , clump_n_trees >= 10 & clump_n_trees <= 15 ~ "10-15 trees"
        # , clump_n_trees > 15 ~ ">15 trees"
        # # , T ~ "error"
      ) %>%
      factor(
        ordered = T
        , levels = c("Individual", "2-4 trees", "5-10 trees", ">10 trees")
        # , levels = c("Individual", "2-4 trees", "5-9 trees", "10-15 trees", ">15 trees")
      )
    ) %>%
    dplyr::ungroup()
  # add back to clumps
  clump_polys <- clump_polys %>% 
    dplyr::inner_join(
      trees_with_clumps %>% 
        sf::st_drop_geometry() %>% 
        dplyr::distinct(clump_id, clump_n_trees, clump_n_trees_grp)
      , by = "clump_id"
    )
  return(list(clump_polys = clump_polys, tree_list = trees_with_clumps))
}

Under this condition, trees that do not have interlocking crowns based on UAS delineations but are within 1 m of one another will be considered part of the same tree clump group. We’ll use this same 1 m distance threshold in defining treeless openings which will be required to comprise continuous space at least 1 m away from the nearest tree.

6.2 Identify Openings

Let’s identify the gaps within a treatment unit. We’ll identify openings based on their distance from the nearest tree crown. We’ll use this same 1 m distance threshold in defining treeless openings, where each opening will be required to comprise continuous space at least 1 m away from the nearest tree. This 1 m buffer accounts for the zone of immediate canopy influence and ensures that areas categorized as open are functionally distinct from the canopy. This threshold is flexible and can be adjusted to meet specific management or analysis objectives (e.g. flame length; wildlife corridors).

First, we’ll generate a distance raster where each cell represents the distance to the nearest tree clump group (i.e. crown) within the treatment unit boundary. Areas with a distance value of 1 m or greater are classified as openings and are defined by spatial connectedness, not a specific geometric shape. As such, they may be irregularly shaped as they are allowed to wind between clumps to follow a path where trees are distant. A single opening continues as long as the flow of open space is not entirely obstructed by surrounding tree groups. Once a gap is completely surrounded by canopy, the opening is closed off and is treated as a discrete spatial feature.

# create function to pass a return from get_clump_summary() and get a distance raster
get_clump_dist_rast_temp <- function(x, clump_dist_m = 3){
  # get clump_dist_m
  # clump_dist_m <- min(get_clump_summary_ans$clump_dist_m, na.rm = T)
  # rasterize the clump polygons and then calculate distance between clumps as raster
  dist_rast <-
    terra::rasterize(
        x = 
          crowns_sf %>% 
          dplyr::inner_join(
            unq_units_df %>% dplyr::slice(x)
            , by = dplyr::join_by(trtmnt_type,trtmnt_block,trtmnt_unit)
          ) %>% 
          terra::vect()
        , y = 
          # crowns_sf %>% 
          # dplyr::inner_join(
          #   unq_units_df %>% dplyr::slice(x)
          #   , by = dplyr::join_by(trtmnt_type,trtmnt_block,trtmnt_unit)
          # ) %>% 
          # terra::vect() %>%
          stand_boundary %>% 
          dplyr::filter(
            unit == unq_units_df$trtmnt_unit[x]
            , block == unq_units_df$trtmnt_block[x]
            , stringr::str_squish(tolower(tretmnt)) == unq_units_df$trtmnt_type[x]
          ) %>% 
          terra::vect() %>% 
          terra::rast(res = 0.2)
      ) %>% 
      terra::distance() %>% 
      # crop it to stand extent
      terra::crop(
        stand_boundary %>% 
          dplyr::filter(
            unit == unq_units_df$trtmnt_unit[x]
            , block == unq_units_df$trtmnt_block[x]
            , stringr::str_squish(tolower(tretmnt)) == unq_units_df$trtmnt_type[x]
          ) %>% 
          terra::vect()
        , mask = T
      )
  ######### part 2
  # now create openings vector data
  openings_vect <- 
    dist_rast %>% 
      terra::classify(rcl = c(clump_dist_m,Inf), others = NA, include.lowest = T) %>% 
      terra::as.polygons(na.rm = T) %>% 
      sf::st_as_sf() %>% 
      sf::st_cast("POLYGON") %>% 
      dplyr::mutate(layer = dplyr::row_number()) %>% 
      dplyr::mutate(
        openining_area_m2 = sf::st_area(geometry) %>% as.numeric()
        , clump_dist_m = clump_dist_m
      )
  
  # return
  return(list(dist_rast = dist_rast, openings_vect = openings_vect))
}

get_clump_n_trees_grp_summary_temp = function(x){
  # get area of harvest unit
  #...will use this area in the area calculations such that...
  #...TPA = trees in a certain group size across the whole stand area
  harvest_area_ha <- 
    stand_boundary %>% 
    dplyr::filter(
      unit == unq_units_df$trtmnt_unit[x]
      , block == unq_units_df$trtmnt_block[x]
      , stringr::str_squish(tolower(tretmnt)) == unq_units_df$trtmnt_type[x]
    ) %>% 
    sf::st_transform(terra::crs(chm_rast)) %>% 
    sf::st_union() %>% 
    sf::st_as_sf() %>% 
    dplyr::mutate(harvest_area_ha = sf::st_area(.) %>% as.numeric() %>% `/`(10000)) %>% 
    dplyr::pull(harvest_area_ha) %>% 
    .[1]
  # which dta
  get_tree_clumps_ans <- 
    crowns_sf %>% 
    sf::st_drop_geometry() %>% 
    dplyr::ungroup() %>% 
    dplyr::inner_join(
      unq_units_df %>% dplyr::slice(x)
      , by = dplyr::join_by(trtmnt_type,trtmnt_block,trtmnt_unit)
    ) %>% 
    dplyr::mutate(stand_area_ha = harvest_area_ha)
  # collapse and calculate silv metrics
  dta <- 
    get_tree_clumps_ans %>% 
    dplyr::group_by(trtmnt_type,trtmnt_block,trtmnt_unit,stand_area_ha,clump_n_trees_grp) %>%
    dplyr::summarise(
      # summary metrics
      n_trees = dplyr::n_distinct(treeID)
      , mean_dbh_cm = mean(dbh_cm, na.rm = T)
      , mean_tree_height_m = mean(tree_height_m, na.rm = T)
      , mean_crown_diameter_m = mean(crown_diameter_m, na.rm = T)
      # , loreys_height_m = sum(basal_area_m2*tree_height_m, na.rm = T) / sum(basal_area_m2, na.rm = T)
      # , basal_area_m2 = sum(basal_area_m2, na.rm = T)
      # , sum_dbh_cm_sq = sum(dbh_cm^2, na.rm = T)
      , .groups = "drop_last"
    ) %>%
    dplyr::ungroup() %>% 
    dplyr::mutate(
      trees_per_ha = (n_trees/stand_area_ha) # (n_trees/clump_area_ha) ... this was not right
      # , basal_area_m2_per_ha = (basal_area_m2/stand_area_ha) # (basal_area_m2/clump_area_ha) ... this was not right
      # , qmd_cm = sqrt(sum_dbh_cm_sq/n_trees)
      # stand calcs
      , stand_trees_per_ha = sum(n_trees)/stand_area_ha
      # , stand_basal_area_m2 = sum(basal_area_m2)
      # , stand_basal_area_m2_per_ha = sum(basal_area_m2)/stand_area_ha
      # , pct_stand_basal_area = basal_area_m2/stand_basal_area_m2
      , pct_stand_n_trees = n_trees/sum(n_trees)
      # , stand_qmd_cm = sqrt(sum(get_tree_clumps_ans$dbh_cm^2, na.rm = T)/sum(n_trees))
    ) %>%
    # dplyr::select(-c(sum_dbh_cm_sq)) %>% 
    # convert to imperial units
    calc_imperial_units_fn()
  # fill in missing
  ret_dta <- 
    dplyr::tibble(
      # get all possible labels from the factor levels
      clump_n_trees_grp = levels(crowns_sf$clump_n_trees_grp)
    ) %>% 
    dplyr::mutate(
      dplyr::across(
        dplyr::all_of(c("clump_n_trees_grp"))
        , ~ factor(
          .x
          , levels = levels(crowns_sf[[dplyr::cur_column()]])
          , ordered = T
        )
      )
    ) %>% 
    dplyr::bind_cols(unq_units_df %>% dplyr::slice(x)) %>% 
    dplyr::left_join(
      dta
      , by = dplyr::join_by(trtmnt_type,trtmnt_block,trtmnt_unit,clump_n_trees_grp)
    ) %>% 
    dplyr::mutate(
      dplyr::across(
        tidyselect::starts_with("stand_")
        , ~ max(.x, na.rm = T)
      )
      , dplyr::across(
        dplyr::where(is.numeric)
        , ~ dplyr::coalesce(.x, 0)
      )
    )
  # return
  return(ret_dta)
}

6.4 Openings Summary

lastly, we’ll look at the openings information.

we define openings as contiguous spatial areas that originate 1 m away from the nearest tree crown. Although these openings may form highly sinuous (“wavy”) corridors winding through tree clump groups, they are closed-off wherever the flow of open space is entirely obstructed by surrounding tree groups. Once a gap is completely surrounded by canopy, the opening is closed off and is treated as a discrete spatial feature. We also require that an opening must have an area greater than 40.5 square meters (0.01 acres) to be considered an actual opening.

6.5 Treatment Compliance Evaluation

The spatially-explicit forest inventories generated with UAS data and individual tree detection point cloud processing workflows enable evaluation of silvicultral treatment prescription compliance. For this specific project, the “uniform” thinning treatments provided explicit instructions for the spatial arrangement of post-treatment stand conditions:

Uniform - Low

Light intensity thinning: Leave 15 feet between tree crowns. 70% of the retained (i.e. leave) trees should be over 8 inches basal diameter if present and 30% should be between 2 inches and 8 inches in basal diameter.

Uniform - High

Heavy intensity thinning: Leave at least 30 feet between tree crowns; 70% of the retained (i.e. leave) trees should be over 8 inches basal diameter if present and 30% should be between 2 inches and 8 inches in basal diameter.

The following is a subset of items provided in the general guidance for both “uniform” prescriptions that are most relevant for our evaluation:

  1. Cut all dead pinyon trees <8 inches in basal diameter. Leave all other standing dead trees for wildlife snags. Dead trees are not considered when applying the spacing guides above.
  2. Remove 70% of all woody shrubs (i.e. gambel oak and serviceberry); retain all gambel oak stems greater than 3 inches in basal diameter.
  3. All trees with green spray paint should be not be cut and piles should be placed at least 15 ft from the canopy of those trees. These have a small piece of equipment that are recording tree growth (Fig. 2). These point dendrometers are on the bole (main stem) of the tree, located 1-2 feet above the ground on the east side of the tree, and a shapefile of the trees approximate location has been provided.
  4. In addition to the above, the following apply: ….
  1. Except for leave trees, all live trees having a minimum size of one (1) inch basal diameter and a height of 12 inches shall be cut.
  2. Retain trees that contain raptor nests.
  3. No ponderosa pine trees should be cut

Since we know our UAS-based individual tree delineation does not perfectly align with field-marked trees (62% F-score), we’ll combine UAS-delineated tree crowns that are spatially continuous (i.e. directly touching or overlapping) into singular “tree objects” for this evaluation of the treatment tree spacing compliance. We’ll then calculate the distance between spatially distinct post treatment tree objects for comparison against the prescription targets. Additionally, since the prescription requires that priority trees like ponderosa pine and wildlife trees are not removed, and thus excluded from the spacing guidelines, the average space between residual trees should be slightly lower than the targets (even if the spacing is perfectly implemented to prescription) due to these restrictions which require keeping more trees than would be needed to meet the spacing guidelines.

As a reminder, we are only evaluating UAS-detected tree objects that have a minimum height of 2 m and have a top point that falls within a treatment unit boundary. This approach assumes that trees within a unit do not form functional clusters with those in adjacent areas (even though some units share a common border and we have data extending beyond the unit boundaries). This assumption prevents data from untreated control areas or differently treated areas from influencing the results when summarized by treatment type. This intentional ignorance of edge effects means that the scale of focus of this analysis is strictly on the treatment unit. As such, we’ll limit the analysis to only portions of crowns that fall within the treatment unit boundary (excluding crown portions that are outside of the unit boundary).

6.5.4.1 Prescription Target Threshold Testing

Finally, let’s perform a one-sided hypothesis test to determine if specific spacing targets were successfully met for the uniform treatments. Rather than comparing treatments to each other, this analysis evaluates each individual treatment’s estimated marginal mean (accounting for both unit size and block variation) against the prescription tree spacing target. The null hypothesis is that the true average tree spacing is less than or equal to the target. A statistically significant upper-tail p-value (p < 0.05) provides evidence that a given treatment reliably met the prescription spacing target
